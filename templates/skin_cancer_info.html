Overview

What is Skin Cancer?

Skin cancer occurs when abnormal skin cells grow uncontrollably. It can appear as a new spot, a changing mole, a non-healing sore, or an unusual lesion. Early detection improves the chance of successful treatment.

This page is for educational purposes only and does not replace professional medical diagnosis.

Major Types

Common Skin Lesion Categories

SkinNova focuses on seven important lesion classes used in dermoscopic AI research.

Melanoma

A dangerous skin cancer type that can spread quickly if not detected early.

High Risk

Melanocytic Nevus

Common mole-like lesion, usually benign but should be monitored for changes.

Low Risk

Basal Cell Carcinoma

A common skin cancer that often grows slowly but needs medical attention.

Moderate Risk

Actinic Keratosis

A rough, scaly lesion caused by sun damage and may become cancerous.

Moderate Risk

Benign Keratosis

A non-cancerous lesion that may visually resemble other skin diseases.

Low Risk

Dermatofibroma

A usually harmless skin growth that appears as a firm small bump.

Low Risk

Vascular Lesion

Lesions related to blood vessels, often red, purple, or blue in color.

Low Risk
Warning Signs

ABCDE Rule for Skin Lesion Screening

The ABCDE rule helps identify suspicious mole or lesion changes.

A

Asymmetry

One half of the lesion does not match the other half.

B

Border

Irregular, blurred, or uneven lesion borders.

C

Color

Multiple colors or uneven pigmentation inside the lesion.

D

Diameter

Lesion larger than 6 mm or increasing in size.

E

Evolution

Any change in size, shape, color, bleeding, or symptoms.
